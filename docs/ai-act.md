# EU AI Act: what applies to Ringbolt, and what ships because of it

Determined 2026-08-24 against Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. Article 50's transparency obligations have
applied since 2 August 2026 (Article 113), verified on
[artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/) on the
date above.

Ringbolt telephones a person, holds a conversation with them, and acts on what they say. That is
the textbook case for one of these obligations and worth being exact about the other three.

## Article 50(1), interaction: APPLIES

> "Providers shall ensure that AI systems intended to interact directly with natural persons are
> designed and developed in such a way that the natural persons concerned are informed that they
> are interacting with an AI system, unless this is obvious..."

Ringbolt is intended to interact directly with a natural person, by telephone, at the worst moment
of their week. The "unless this is obvious" carve-out is not available: the product's entire
argument against every other on-call tool is that its calls are a real conversation rather than a
recording with keypad codes, and somebody woken at three in the morning is in no position to work
out what they are speaking to.

**What ships.** The opening sentence of every call says it. `AUTOMATED_DISCLOSURE` in
`src/domain/brief.ts` is one of the instructions carried in every task brief, before the incident
facts, and it is written in the words a half-awake person understands rather than a legalism they
have to decode. There is no configuration, no service policy and no action that removes it.
`src/domain/brief.test.ts` asserts it is present, that it comes before the facts, and that it
survives a call offering nothing at all. Dropping it reddens those tests, which was checked by
dropping it.

The obligation names the provider, which is whoever puts Ringbolt into service under their own
name. Shipping the disclosure in the brief rather than documenting it as an operator's duty is what
makes the obligation met by default instead of met by somebody remembering.

## Article 50(2), marking synthetic output: falls on CALL-E, not here

50(2) binds providers of AI systems that generate synthetic audio, image, video or text, and
requires the output be marked machine-readably as artificially generated. The synthetic speech on a
Ringbolt call is generated by CALL-E's system, and CALL-E is its provider. Ringbolt supplies the
words to say and reads back the transcript; it does not generate the audio.

Ringbolt publishes none of that audio or text anywhere. Transcripts are stored as the evidence
behind a production change, readable only behind `ADMIN_TOKEN`.

## Article 50(3), emotion recognition and biometric categorisation: does not apply

Ringbolt runs no emotion recognition and no biometric categorisation. The confidence score it reads
is CALL-E's own certainty about whether the task was completed, not a claim about the person's
emotional state, and it is used only to refuse a decision that is not certain enough to act on.

This one would change if a later version scored how a responder sounded. It does not, and if that
is ever proposed, 50(3) is the reason to think hard about it.

## Article 50(4), deep fakes and public-interest text: does not apply

Ringbolt does not imitate any real person. It says it is Ringbolt and that it is automated, which
is the opposite of the thing 50(4) is about. It publishes no text intended to inform the public on
matters of public interest.

## Not an Article 50 question, but recorded here so it is not mistaken for one

Ringbolt is not a high-risk system under Annex III. It is not used in employment decisions, credit,
education, essential services eligibility, law enforcement, migration, or justice. It calls an
engineer about a computer that is broken.

The personal data questions, which are GDPR rather than the AI Act, are the phone numbers and the
transcripts. Retention and deletion are phase 7 and are named in the roadmap, not left implied.
